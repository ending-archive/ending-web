--- I. The Walls ---

A quiet room is never silent. Listen long enough and the refrigerator hums its single note, the pipes tick as they cool, a neighbour's footsteps cross the ceiling like slow weather.

What we call quiet is really the absence of demand. No one is asking anything of us. The sounds that remain are the building's own, and they keep us company without requiring an answer.

--- II. The Light ---

Light in a quiet room moves more slowly than elsewhere, or we simply notice it more. A rectangle crosses the floor in the afternoon, climbs the far wall, and thins to nothing by evening.

Watch it for a week and you will have a clock more honest than any on the wall.

--- III. The Chair ---

Every quiet room has one chair that is really a decision: the place where someone chose to sit and not be reached. Its cushion remembers a particular weight. Its angle favours a particular window.

To sit in it is to borrow that decision, and to find, usually to our surprise, that we agree with it.
