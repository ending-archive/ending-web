--- The Ink Room ---

Mira learned to read maps before she learned to read words. Her father kept a room at the top of the house where the light came in sideways and the air smelled of gall ink and warm paper. She was allowed to sit under the great table as long as she did not touch the brass dividers.

"A map is a promise," he told her once, without looking up. "It says: if you go here, you will find this. The trouble is that the world does not keep promises."

She did not understand him then. She only watched his hand draw a coastline in one long, unbroken stroke, and thought it was the most certain thing she had ever seen.

The winter she turned twelve, he stopped drawing coastlines. He drew the same small island again and again, and each time he rubbed it out.

--- The Island That Wasn't ---

After the funeral, Mira found the island in his last sketchbook, drawn forty times and named once: Ilha Perdida, in a hand so faint it might have been a smudge.

The Admiralty had no record of it. The harbour master laughed. An old pilot at the quay said only that there were places the sea kept for itself, and that her father had been a fool to try to pin one down.

She bought a second-hand sextant that evening. It cost more than she had, so she sold the dividers, the brass ones she had never been allowed to touch, and felt nothing at all, which frightened her more than grief would have.

--- Dead Reckoning ---

Eleven days out, the stars were exactly where the charts said they should be and the island was nowhere.

Mira sat in the stern with the sketchbook open on her knees and finally understood what her father had meant. The map had never promised the island. It had promised her father, standing at a table, certain of one stroke at a time.

At dawn a line of green rose out of the haze, low and ordinary. She did not write it down. She simply steered toward it, and for the first time in her life she let the world keep its own counsel.
