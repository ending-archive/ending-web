--- First Letter: On Looking ---

Dear Ines,

You ask where one begins. Begin with one plant, and begin by sitting beside it for an hour without a notebook. You will be tempted to name it quickly. Resist. A name is a door closing; first let the door stand open.

Notice which way the leaves turn in the afternoon. Notice what visits it, and what ignores it. When you finally do write, write what you saw, not what you already knew. The two are rarely the same, and the gap between them is where discoveries live.

Yours in patience,
H. T. M.

--- Second Letter: On Failure ---

Dear Ines,

Your seedlings died. Good. Not good that they died, but good that you are writing to me about it instead of pretending otherwise.

I have killed, by my own count, some four hundred specimens in forty years. Each death taught me something a living plant could not: which soil was too sour, which window too kind, which impatience of mine mattered most.

Keep a ledger of your failures, as carefully as your successes. In a decade it will be the more valuable book.

Yours,
H. T. M.

--- Third Letter: On Patience ---

Dear Ines,

The oak outside my window was an acorn the year Napoleon was exiled. It has not once hurried on my account.

You are twenty-three and you want results. Of course you do. But consider that the organism you study keeps a calendar longer than your career, and is entirely unbothered by your deadlines.

Learn to work on its time. It is the only humility that science truly requires.

Yours always,
H. T. M.
