Chapter 1

The teacup on the shelf had been chipped for thirty years, and nobody had thrown it away. Hana noticed this the morning after her mother's funeral, when the house was finally quiet enough to notice anything.

There were many such objects. A cracked ruler. A scarf with one frayed end. A jar of buttons that matched no coat. They sat in their places and said nothing, and the nothing was enormous.

Chapter 2

She began to touch them, one a day. Not to sort or discard, only to hold. The ruler was warmer than she expected. The scarf still held a trace of camphor.

On the ninth day she found a button she recognised from her own school uniform, sewn back on crookedly by hands that had never been good at sewing. She sat on the floor of the hallway and laughed until it turned into something else.

Chapter 3

In spring she opened every window. The house smelled of rain and cut grass, and the objects looked different in the new light, less like relics, more like company.

She kept the teacup. She drank from it every morning, careful of the chip, and the silence in the house learned to sound a little like peace.
