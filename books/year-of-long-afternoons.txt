--- Spring ---

In the village the afternoons began after lunch and ended whenever the church bell lost patience. Marco, who had come to sell his late uncle's house, found that nobody was in a hurry for him to finish.

"Sit," said Signora Rinaldi from her doorstep, and pointed at a chair that was not there yet. A boy appeared with it a few minutes later, as if the chair and the invitation had been negotiated in advance.

--- Summer ---

He did not sell the house in summer. He repaired the shutters, learned which neighbour's tomatoes were the sweetest and which neighbour's opinions were the loudest, and discovered that the two were rarely the same person.

The light lay across the piazza like something spilled. Time, which in the city had been a thing he spent, here seemed to be a thing he was simply allowed to stand in.

--- Autumn ---

The estate agent called twice. Marco let the phone ring against the kitchen table and watched a wasp circle the grapes.

When he finally answered, he heard himself say that the house was no longer for sale, and was surprised only by how little surprise there was in it.
